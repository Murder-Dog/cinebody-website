<p>There's a lot of noise right now about platforms &quot;cracking down&quot; on AI video. Some of it is true and a lot of it is exaggerated. Here's what the big platforms have actually changed, what they haven't, and what it means if your brand publishes video.</p>
<h2>The short answer</h2>
<p>No major platform has banned AI video. What they are doing is <strong>labeling</strong> it, and in some cases <strong>not paying</strong> for it. Labels matter more than they sound. A label changes how people read your video before they've watched a second of it.</p>
<h2>YouTube</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Disclosure is required.</strong> Since 2024, creators have had to disclose realistic video that was altered or made with AI, especially anything showing a real-looking person, place, or event. YouTube then shows a label. Creators who repeatedly skip it can face penalties, including losing monetization.</li>
<li><strong>Mass-produced content doesn't get paid.</strong> In July 2025, YouTube renamed its &quot;repetitious content&quot; rule to <strong>&quot;inauthentic content.&quot;</strong> On July 13, 2026, it spelled out three kinds of content that can't earn money: generic or template-made videos, content built on manipulative or shock-for-views formulas, and AI personas posing as experts on health, legal, money, or political topics.</li>
<li><strong>It isn't an AI ban.</strong> YouTube says the rules themselves didn't change, and AI-assisted videos that add real value can still earn. It reviews a channel as a whole, not one video at a time.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Meta (Facebook, Instagram, Threads)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>&quot;AI info&quot; labels.</strong> Since May 2024, Meta has labeled content when it detects industry-standard signals that AI was used, or when the person posting says so.</li>
<li><strong>It reads the file's credentials.</strong> Meta looks for the &quot;AI generated&quot; markers that tools from Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Adobe, and others build into their files, using the C2PA and IPTC standards.</li>
<li><strong>You're expected to disclose.</strong> Realistic video or audio that was made or altered digitally is supposed to be labeled when you post it. AI in ads is allowed, but disclosure rules apply.</li>
</ul>
<h2>TikTok</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Automatic labels.</strong> In May 2024, TikTok became one of the first big platforms to label AI-generated content automatically when a file carries Content Credentials, even if it was made somewhere else.</li>
<li><strong>Creators must label realistic AI.</strong> AI content showing realistic people or scenes needs TikTok's AI-generated label.</li>
</ul>
<h2>And now it's the law in Europe</h2>
<p>The EU AI Act's transparency rules took effect on <strong>August 2, 2026</strong>. Anyone publishing a deepfake, meaning AI-made or AI-altered content that looks like real people, places, or events and could pass as real, has to label it clearly. That applies to brands running campaigns in Europe, not just to the tech companies.</p>
<h2>What this means for brands</h2>
<p>Put it together and the direction is clear, even if the rules aren't identical:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Generated video gets labeled.</strong> More and more often, the platform does it for you whether or not you disclose.</li>
<li><strong>Mass-produced video loses money.</strong> At least on YouTube, template content isn't worth paying for.</li>
<li><strong>Proof of real is getting built in.</strong> Apple and Google are adding capture signatures to their cameras (<a href="/cinebody-blog/apple-reference-image-proof-of-real">more on Apple's new Reference Image</a>).</li>
</ol>
<p>So &quot;can we use AI?&quot; is the wrong question. The better one is <strong>&quot;could we show where this video came from?&quot;</strong> If the answer is yes, labels aren't your problem. If the answer is &quot;a prompt,&quot; you'll want to disclose, and plan for how your audience reacts.</p>
<h2>A quick gut check</h2>
<p>Before you publish, ask:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Is there a real person on camera?</strong> Or a generated face or voice standing in for one?</li>
<li><strong>Could you prove it?</strong> Is there a record of who filmed it, when, and on what?</li>
<li><strong>Would a label change how it lands?</strong> A testimonial with an &quot;AI info&quot; tag isn't much of a testimonial.</li>
</ul>
