<p>When your employees, customers, and fans film for your brand, they are doing real work. How you thank them shapes who shows up, what they film, and how much of it you can use. Get it wrong and you either get three clips or three hundred you cannot use.</p>
<p>There is no single right answer. There are three common models, each with a job it does well, plus a few rules that apply no matter which one you pick.</p>
<h2>Flat rate: pay for taking part</h2>
<p>A flat rate pays each person a set amount for filming the project.</p>
<p><strong>It fits when</strong> you have a defined group and a clear list: store staff filming a launch, a team of brand ambassadors, a few customers you invited by name. You want complete coverage of the shot list, not volume.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for</strong> a fuzzy definition of &quot;done.&quot; Someone who sends one clip and someone who sends every shot get the same pay. Say up front what counts as delivered, such as &quot;at least one usable clip for each shot.&quot;</p>
<h2>Per-clip bounty: pay for what you use</h2>
<p>A bounty pays for each approved clip.</p>
<p><strong>It fits when</strong> you are running an open call to a big community and variety matters: lots of cities, lots of faces, lots of angles. People who film more and film well earn more.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for</strong> quantity over quality. A bounty with no bar invites a flood of near-identical takes. Set a quality bar for approval, cap how much one person can earn on a project, and consider a bonus for standout clips so the incentive points at good footage, not more footage.</p>
<h2>Gift cards, product, and perks</h2>
<p>Not every project needs cash. A gift card, free product, early access, or a prize for the top filmer can be the right thank-you.</p>
<p><strong>It fits when</strong> the people filming are fans and customers who would do it for the love of the brand, the budget is small, or the shoot is a one-off event.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out for</strong> two things. First, a gift card or free product is still something of value, which matters for disclosure (more below). Second, if the prize goes to the &quot;best&quot; clip, write down how you will pick, and be aware that contests and prizes can come with their own rules depending on where people live.</p>
<h2>Keep it fair and clear</h2>
<p>Whatever you choose, the basics are the same:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Say what it pays before anyone films.</strong> No surprises after the work is done.</li>
<li><strong>Define approved.</strong> Tell people what makes a clip usable, and share the shot list so they know what you need.</li>
<li><strong>Say when they get paid.</strong> Then stick to it.</li>
<li><strong>Be clear about rights.</strong> People should know how their footage will be used and where. Start with <a href="/cinebody-blog/what-are-usage-rights">What Are Usage Rights?</a></li>
<li><strong>Pay for the filming, not the opinion.</strong> Never tie a reward to saying something positive. Beyond being bad practice, the FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials bans incentives that require a particular sentiment.</li>
</ul>
<p>A general note on money and paperwork: paying individuals can create tax reporting obligations for you and taxable income for them, and paying your own employees extra for filming may belong in payroll. This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Talk to your accountant or lawyer about your situation.</p>
<h2>Disclose when paid people appear in your ads</h2>
<p>This is the part brands most often miss. Under the FTC's Endorsement Guides, if there is a connection between the person on camera and your brand that viewers would not expect, it should be disclosed clearly. Payment counts. Free or discounted product counts. Employment counts.</p>
<p>In practice:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Put the disclosure where people will see it.</strong> For video, that means in the video itself, not buried in a caption or a profile.</li>
<li><strong>Use plain words.</strong> &quot;Ad,&quot; &quot;Sponsored,&quot; or &quot;Thanks to [Brand] for the free product&quot; work. Vague tags like &quot;sp&quot; or &quot;collab&quot; do not.</li>
<li><strong>Employees disclose too.</strong> Having the company in their profile is not enough, because viewers of a single post will not see it.</li>
<li><strong>It is on you as well.</strong> The FTC expects brands to explain disclosure rules to the people endorsing them and to keep an eye on what gets posted.</li>
</ul>
<p>The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, in effect since October 21, 2024, adds two points that matter here: insiders such as employees and managers must clearly disclose their relationship when they give testimonials, and you cannot make an incentive depend on a positive (or negative) message.</p>
<p>Disclosure does not make community video less credible. A real customer saying &quot;they sent me the bike, here is what I actually think&quot; still beats a stranger reading a script.</p>
<h2>How payouts work in Cinebody</h2>
<p>In Cinebody, you choose the model for each project:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Flat fee:</strong> a set amount per filmer. You pay it to each person who delivered for the project.</li>
<li><strong>Per-clip bounty:</strong> a set amount per approved clip, with an optional bonus for high-scoring clips and an optional cap per filmer. A clip earns when it is approved, whether you approve it or Cinebody's clip review approves it under the sensitivity you set.</li>
<li><strong>No cash payout:</strong> write your own incentive, like &quot;Top filmer gets a gift card,&quot; and it shows on the project. You deliver that reward yourself.</li>
</ul>
<p>You fund the budget up front, and the card processing fee is added on top so the full budget reaches filmers. Filmers see what the project pays before they film.</p>
<p>Earnings wait for your approval. Once you approve, the money is available to the filmer, who connects Stripe once in the Cinebody app and cashes out there. Paid work runs through the Cinebody app: clips filmed in the browser are free to collect, but they do not earn.</p>
<p>Real people film it. Cinebody coaches the shoot, scores each clip, and cuts the video, and the payout side keeps what people earn tied to the footage you actually use.</p>
