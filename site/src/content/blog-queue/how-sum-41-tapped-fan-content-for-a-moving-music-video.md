<p>Some songs need more than a slick music video. "Catching Fire," Sum 41's 2021 single, deals heavily with mental health and suicide. The band, director John Asher, and their management company, Patriot Management, wanted a video that connected the song to the real experiences of the people listening to it.</p>
<p>So they asked the fans.</p>

<h2>The band and the brief</h2>
<p>Sum 41 has never followed convention. This is the band behind the party anthem "<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CMX2lPum_pg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Fat Lip</a>," which mixes skate punk, nu-metal, and pop. For "Catching Fire," the goal was different: honesty, not spectacle.</p>
<p>Traditional music videos rely on full lighting setups, location scouting, equipment rentals, and a crew. That gives a polished look. It is rarely the best way to capture authentic moments from real people. Those have to come from the fans themselves.</p>
<blockquote><p>"We wanted real people with real stories to create a visual connection between song and experience," said Chris Nary, Manager at Patriot Management. "Cinebody was the perfect way to reach Sum 41's fans and get the genuine footage we needed, and fast."</p></blockquote>

<h2>A hybrid of professional and fan footage</h2>
<p>Patriot Management already had Cinebody in its toolkit, and the team chose a hybrid approach: professionally shot footage combined with footage filmed by fans. The professional side kept the video polished. The fan footage gave it weight.</p>
<p>Artists including OneRepublic and Durand Jones had already built music videos from fan footage. Bands like Sum 41 have loyal followings, and that loyalty was the engine for this shoot.</p>

<h2>One specific ask, with an example</h2>
<p>The project was announced with a quick call to action on social media and a unique join code. Fans could join the project and film as soon as they were ready.</p>
<p>John Asher's shot list had a single, specific request:</p>
<blockquote><p>"Write the name of a loved one that you've lost to suicide on your hand, the date they were born, and the date you lost them, and bring it slowly into the frame."</p></blockquote>
<p>To make it even clearer, Asher uploaded an example video for fans to watch before filming. The instruction told fans exactly what to do. The example showed them how it should look.</p>

<h2>Footage that fit the edit</h2>
<p>Fan submissions started arriving almost immediately after the post went live. Because the project controlled the filming specs on participants' phones, the fan footage dropped straight into the existing post-production workflow alongside the professional material.</p>
<p>The final cut kept its quality, its style, and its authenticity, and made the combination look effortless.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EiNbgTrQCd8" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The "Catching Fire" video</a> passed a quarter of a million YouTube views in under two weeks, and the count kept climbing.</p>

<h2>What other artists and brands can take from it</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Match the method to the message.</strong> A song about real loss called for real people. The format came from the subject.</li>
<li><strong>Make one ask, and make it exact.</strong> A single, precise instruction is easier to follow than a long list, especially for something personal.</li>
<li><strong>Show, then ask.</strong> An example video removes guesswork before anyone presses record.</li>
<li><strong>Plan for the edit up front.</strong> Consistent specs let fan footage sit next to professional footage without a fight in post.</li>
<li><strong>Lower the barrier to join.</strong> A social post and a join code got fans filming the moment they were ready.</li>
</ul>
