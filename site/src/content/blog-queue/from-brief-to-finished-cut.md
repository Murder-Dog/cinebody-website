<p>A typical community video project runs through five or six tools. A doc for the brief, a spreadsheet for the shot list, email for recruiting, a shared drive for uploads, an editing app, and a file-sharing link for delivery. Every handoff drops a little context: which clip was for which shot, which take was the good one, what the client actually asked for.</p>
<p>Cinebody runs the whole production in one place, from the brief to the finished cut. Most tools own one step. Here is one project walked end to end, with an honest line between what happens on its own and what your team still decides.</p>
<p>The example: a bike shop with a handful of locations is launching a new commuter bike. They want a 30-second social video of real customers and staff riding it to work.</p>
<h2>1. The brief</h2>
<p>You write a few plain sentences: what you are making, who is filming, and where it will run. &quot;Staff and customers film their morning commute on the new bike. We need a 30-second vertical video for Instagram and TikTok. Hands on the bike, the ride, and one line about how it feels.&quot;</p>
<p>If you are stuck, Idea Studio deals you shoot ideas built on your brand, and keeping one turns it into a project. Your Brand Kit, drafted from your website, carries your colors, voice, and filming rules into everything that follows.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> the goal, who films, and where it runs.</p>
<h2>2. The shot list</h2>
<p>Cinebody writes the shot list from your description. Every shot gets plain directions a person can follow on a phone, plus the orientation it should be filmed in and whether it wants slow motion. You edit anything, reorder, or add shots by hand.</p>
<p>A short list of clear asks beats a long wish list. We wrote about that in <a href="/cinebody-blog/start-your-shot-list-from-the-brief">Start Your Shot List From a Brief</a>.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> which shots stay, and which one matters most.</p>
<h2>3. Recruiting filmers</h2>
<p>Each project gets a share link and a QR code. Put it on a poster at the register, drop it in the staff group chat, or email it to customers who bought the bike. The page explains the project and gets people filming.</p>
<p>You also set how people are paid, per project: a flat fee, a per-clip bounty, or no cash with an incentive you write, like a gift card for the top filmer. Filmers see what it pays before they start.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> who to invite and what it is worth to them.</p>
<h2>4. Live coaching</h2>
<p>When someone opens the camera, the shot's brief is on screen and the AI Director coaches them while they film: light, focus, steadiness, audio, and the creative calls a director would make out loud. It keeps to one clear note at a time. (More in <a href="/cinebody-blog/inside-the-ai-director">Inside the AI Director</a>.)</p>
<p>For story shots, AI Voice Interviews ask the questions out loud and follow up on the answers, so a customer does not have to invent a testimonial on the spot.</p>
<p>From your desk, you watch clips land on a live map and can message one filmer or all of them. If every clip so far is a wide shot, send a direction to get closer on the bike, and it reaches them while the camera is still up.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> when to step in.</p>
<h2>5. Clips land and get scored</h2>
<p>Each clip is transcribed and scored as it arrives, on how it looks, how it sounds, and how well it answers the shot. You get a plain verdict and a note on what would make it better, and you can search everything by what people said.</p>
<p>Based on a sensitivity you set, clear keepers can be approved and clear misses set aside automatically. Everything in between waits for a person, and you can overrule any call.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> how strict to be, and the close calls.</p>
<h2>6. The first cut</h2>
<p>In Post Production, Cinebody reads all the footage and tells you the story it found before it cuts anything. For the bike launch, that might be: the morning routine, the ride, one line about how it feels. You can push back on the story first.</p>
<p>Then one click builds the cut: the best takes in order, B-roll over the seams, captions, and music. The review from step five matters here. The strongest stretch of each clip is already marked, so the trims start from the best moment instead of guessing.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> whether the story is right.</p>
<h2>7. Revisions by note</h2>
<p>Don't love a part? Type a note like &quot;get to the hook faster&quot; or &quot;end on the rider, not the logo,&quot; and Cinebody revises that cut. You can also work the timeline by hand. Higher tiers export to Premiere, Final Cut, or EDL if your editor wants to finish elsewhere.</p>
<p><strong>You decide:</strong> when it is done.</p>
<h2>8. Delivery with proof</h2>
<p>When the cut renders, it gets a link you can send to the client or your team, and you can take the link down later. Under the video is the Proof of Capture: a list of every source clip in the cut, each with a signed receipt of when it arrived, the phone it came from, and a fingerprint of the untouched original. Anyone can check a receipt. It shows where each file came from, not a judgment of what the footage shows.</p>
<h2>Why connected steps beat stitched tools</h2>
<p>The value is not any one step. It is that each step reads the one before it.</p>
<ul>
<li>The shot list tells the Director what each person is trying to film.</li>
<li>The shot list and your brand guidelines tell the clip review what a good take looks like.</li>
<li>The review tells the editor which takes are strongest and where the best moment in each clip starts and ends.</li>
<li>The receipts from upload travel all the way to delivery.</li>
</ul>
<p>Stitch five tools together and a person has to carry all of that by hand: renaming files, matching clips to shots, rewatching everything to find the good line. That is usually where the week goes.</p>
<h2>What stays human</h2>
<p>Real people film every frame. Cinebody coaches, scores, and cuts, and AI helps with the edit, but it never makes the footage. Your team still owns the parts that need taste: the idea, who gets invited, the close calls, and the final yes.</p>
