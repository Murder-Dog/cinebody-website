<p>Nano influencers, usually defined as creators with roughly 1,000 to 10,000 followers, are easy to picture and hard to find. They do not show up in celebrity rankings, and many of them have never worked with a brand before.</p>
<p>The good news is that the best places to look are often free. Here are eleven, in the order most brands should try them: your own audience first, then the platforms' built-in marketplaces, then paid tools. Once you have a shortlist, <a href="/cinebody-blog/how-to-engage-micro-amp-nano-influencers-in-5-easy-steps">How to Work With Micro and Nano Influencers</a> covers outreach, briefs, pay, and rights.</p>

<h2>Start free: the people already around your brand</h2>
<h3>1. Your own followers and tags</h3>
<p>Someone already posting about you is a nano influencer who needs no convincing. On Instagram, check the tagged posts on your profile, which collect photos and videos you have been tagged in. Then go through your mentions and comments on every platform. Note the accounts that post often, look good on camera, and get real replies from their own followers.</p>
<h3>2. Your customers</h3>
<p>Plenty of customers have a following you do not know about. Look through reviews, loyalty members, and people who email or DM you about the product. A short post or email inviting customers to apply as creators can surface people no search tool would flag.</p>
<h3>3. Hashtag and keyword search</h3>
<p>Search your category, not just your brand name, inside the apps themselves. Instagram hashtag pages collect public posts that use a tag, and TikTok search works the same way for keywords and hashtags. Local and niche tags (a city plus a hobby, a specific trail, a product type) tend to turn up smaller creators than broad ones.</p>

<h2>The platforms' own creator marketplaces</h2>
<p>All three big platforms now run official tools where brands can search for creators and start a collaboration. They are free to browse with a business account. One caveat for nano work: each has eligibility rules for creators, such as an established following, program membership, or region, so smaller accounts are easier to miss here than in steps one through three.</p>
<h3>4. Meta Creator Marketplace</h3>
<p>Built into Meta's business tools, it helps brands discover and evaluate Instagram creators for collaborations and partnership ads, which run from the creator's handle. Meta says creators need an established presence and a sufficient follower base to be included.</p>
<h3>5. TikTok One</h3>
<p>TikTok's official platform for brand and creator collaboration, which grew out of the TikTok Creator Marketplace. Brands can search for creators, browse recommendations, and launch branded content projects. Creator eligibility depends on region, age, and TikTok's content policies.</p>
<h3>6. YouTube Creator Partnerships</h3>
<p>Formerly YouTube BrandConnect, renamed in March 2026. Advertisers use it inside Google Ads and Display and Video 360 to discover creators, build outreach lists, and send inquiries. It only includes creators in the YouTube Partner Program, which favors channels that are already established.</p>

<h2>Paid discovery tools</h2>
<p>These are worth it when you need volume, detailed filters, or help managing many creators at once. Pricing changes often, so check each one directly.</p>
<h3>7. Heepsy</h3>
<p>An influencer search tool for Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. You filter by niche, location, and follower count, then check audience and engagement data before you reach out. Heepsy lets you start for free, which makes it a low-risk first paid-tier tool.</p>
<h3>8. Upfluence</h3>
<p>A creator search and campaign platform with a useful twist for nano work: it connects to your online store and identifies influential people among your existing customers. It also handles outreach, payments, and sales tracking.</p>
<h3>9. GRIN</h3>
<p>Built mainly for ecommerce and direct-to-consumer brands. Along with filtered search across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, it offers lookalike search (find more creators like the ones who worked) and social listening to find people already talking about your brand.</p>
<h3>10. Meltwater Influencer Marketing (formerly Klear)</h3>
<p>Meltwater acquired Klear in 2021 and now sells it as part of its media intelligence suite. It offers creator discovery from a large database, fake-follower and bot checks, and campaign management. A fit for teams that already use Meltwater for social or media monitoring.</p>

<h3>11. Insense</h3>
<p>More a hiring marketplace than a search tool. Insense connects brands with vetted creators, including micro and nano accounts, for content and posts. It links to Meta partnership ads and TikTok Spark Ads, so it suits brands that plan to run creator content as paid social.</p>

<h2>Which place to start</h2>
<p>If you have an audience at all, start with steps one through three. They cost nothing, and the people you find there already like you. Add a native marketplace when you need reach on a specific platform, and a paid tool when you need scale or tighter filters.</p>
