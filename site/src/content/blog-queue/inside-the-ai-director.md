<p>Most footage problems get found in the edit. By then the soft focus, the blown-out window, and the missing sound are baked in, and the person who filmed it has gone home.</p>
<p>The AI Director moves that moment up to while the camera is still rolling. A real person films. Cinebody watches the frame with them and says what to fix while there is still time to fix it. Here is what the filmer sees, what gets checked, and why it talks less than you might expect.</p>
<h2>What the filmer sees when the camera opens</h2>
<p>A filmer taps your link and the camera opens in their browser, no app download. The Cinebody app has the same camera for people who want it.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The shot's brief, on screen.</strong> The plain directions for this shot sit at the top, right where they are needed. The filmer can fold it down to a small tab that still shows the first line.</li>
<li><strong>The right orientation.</strong> Every shot carries its orientation. If a shot is meant to be horizontal and the phone is upright, the camera asks them to turn it before they record.</li>
<li><strong>Four quick checks.</strong> A small row reads Light, Focus, Steady, and Audio. Each stays green when it is fine and changes color when it slips.</li>
<li><strong>One Director note.</strong> A single line from the Director, when there is something worth saying.</li>
</ul>
<p>In the app, a framing grid is on by default, which helps with the basics in <a href="/cinebody-blog/how-to-frame-video-on-smartphones">How to Frame Video on Your Phone</a>.</p>
<h2>What it checks</h2>
<p>The Director works on two levels at once.</p>
<p><strong>The technical basics run on the phone.</strong> Light, focus, steadiness, and sound are measured right on the device, so the checks respond instantly and keep working even when the connection is weak. In the app, sound is checked while the filmer sets up, before they press record, so the check never competes with the recording itself.</p>
<p><strong>Framing and the creative call come from the Director.</strong> Cinebody looks at what the camera sees alongside the shot's brief, its required orientation, and your brand guidelines. Before recording, it helps the filmer find the spot and the angle: better light by the window, a cleaner background, a step closer. Once they are rolling, it directs the take.</p>
<p>It also knows the brief is a starting point, not a checklist. If the filmer finds something better than what was written, the Director lets them run with it instead of nagging them back to the literal words.</p>
<h2>One clear note, not a pile of warnings</h2>
<p>A camera that flashes five warnings at once teaches people to ignore all of them. So everything that wants the filmer's attention goes through one slot on screen, labeled Director.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The most important note wins.</strong> A real problem goes ahead of a style suggestion.</li>
<li><strong>Notes stay put long enough to read.</strong> A new note does not flicker in over the last one. Old notes clear on their own, so a stale tip never sits on the screen.</li>
<li><strong>Normal dips stay quiet.</strong> The four checks already show small changes in light or steadiness. The Director only speaks up when something is actually wrecking the shot, like a subject in full silhouette, a smudged lens, or the phone held the wrong way, and it says it once.</li>
<li><strong>No repeats, no contradictions.</strong> It keeps track of what it just said, so it does not suggest the plant in the corner and then call the plant distracting.</li>
<li><strong>Praise is earned.</strong> When the filmer nails a frame, it tells them. No empty cheerleading.</li>
</ul>
<p>Silence is part of the design. While filming, if the shot is working, the Director stays quiet. No note and a row of green checks means keep going.</p>
<h2>Directions from your team, mid-shoot</h2>
<p>Coaching is not only automatic. From your desk, you watch clips land on a live map as people film, and you can message one filmer or all of them.</p>
<p>You can also send a direction mid-shoot. Say every clip so far is a wide shot. Send a direction to get closer on the product, and it reaches the filmer while the camera is still up. It shows in that same Director spot, so the filmer hears one voice with one next step, not a chat thread competing with the coaching. (For how that fits the whole project, see <a href="/cinebody-blog/from-brief-to-finished-cut">From Brief to Finished Cut</a>.)</p>
<h2>What it never gets in the way of</h2>
<p>Coaching is help, not a gate. Filming never waits on it.</p>
<ul>
<li>If the connection drops, the filmer keeps recording. In the app, the Director says coaching is offline and that the checks still work, because those run on the phone.</li>
<li>In the app, the filmer can turn the Director off. The checks keep showing.</li>
<li>For interview shots in the app, Cinebody's AI Voice Interviews take over the talking, and the interviewer's tips appear in the same Director spot. The basics of a good interview setup are in <a href="/cinebody-blog/5-steps-to-recording-interviews-iphone">Record a Great iPhone Interview</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>And the Director never makes the footage. It does not add faces, voices, or anything that was not in front of the lens. Everything it coaches is a real person filming a real moment.</p>
<h2>Why coaching while filming beats fixing it in post</h2>
<p>Editing can do a lot. It cannot rescue a face that is out of focus, a line drowned out by wind, or a vertical clip that needed to be horizontal. Color correction can lift a dark shot a little, and stabilization can smooth some shake, but both cost quality and time. A true fix means a reshoot, which means finding the same person, the same place, and the same light again.</p>
<p>A note during the take costs the filmer a few seconds. Move toward the window. Hold steady. Get closer. The clip that comes back is one the editor can actually use, which matters most for B-roll, where small problems add up fast (more in <a href="/cinebody-blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-capturing-b-roll">Film B-Roll Editors Can Actually Use</a>).</p>
<p>The same things the Director checks live get reviewed again once the clip lands: focus, exposure, stability, audio, and framing, plus a plain verdict. That is how the pieces connect. The shot list tells the Director what each person is trying to film, the Director helps them film it, and the review scores what came back. The details are in <a href="/cinebody-blog/how-cinebody-scores-every-clip">How Cinebody Scores Every Clip</a>.</p>
<p>The filmer stays in charge the whole time. They pick the moment, point the phone, and decide when a take is done. Cinebody just makes sure a good director is standing next to them.</p>
